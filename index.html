Hollywood keeps reinventing itself. What happens next?

Hollywood Evolves traces how the industry’s tools, talent, and business models have repeatedly reinvented themselves. Each episode pairs a voice from the last technology shift with an operator building the next one—then asks both to put a number on what happens next.

01 / The format

Three acts. Two guests. One forecast.

Every episode is recorded with Ian and two expert guests: one who can ground the topic in Hollywood’s historical arc, and one actively operating in today’s market.

  • The hostIan McPherson frames the technology shift.
  • The historical guestGrounds the topic in Hollywood’s institutional arc.
  • The operating guestShapes today’s market or technology.
  1. 5 minIntroduction

    Ian introduces the guests and the episode’s innovation theme, framing the technology shift under discussion.

  2. 10 minAct I — The Past

    The historical guest explains the prior pivot: what changed, and what leap in quality or access it unlocked.

  3. 10 minAct II — The Present

    The operating guest analyzes how today’s technology is reshaping the relationship between content, creator, and customer.

  4. 10 minAct III — Future Synthesis

    Both guests commit to a probability forecast on a defined scenario; the audience casts a Community Forecast against it.

04 / The prediction market

Expert Alpha versus the crowd.

Every finale turns speculation into a number. Each forecast question is developed with DEG sub-committees around a theme that matters to studio, platform, technology, and creative leaders.

  1. 01Expert Alpha

    Both guests put a professional percentage forecast on the episode’s defined scenario.

  2. 02Community Forecast

    The audience forecasts the same question on the same terms, so expert and crowd consensus sit side by side.

  3. 03Market Update

    Later episodes revisit earlier questions, show how consensus has moved, and grade each forecast against the outcome.

The aim is calibration, not crowning winners: a recurring, measurable read on where the industry thinks it is headed—and how well it called the last turn.
