Hollywood keeps reinventing itself. What happens next?

Hollywood Evolves traces how the industry’s tools, talent, and business models have repeatedly reinvented themselves. Each episode pairs a voice from the last technology shift with an operator building the next one—then asks both to put a number on what happens next.

01 / The format

Three acts. Two guests. One forecast.

Every episode is recorded with Ian and two expert guests: one who can ground the topic in Hollywood’s historical arc, and one actively operating in today’s market.

  • The hostIan McPherson frames the technology shift.
  • The historical guestGrounds the topic in Hollywood’s institutional arc.
  • The operating guestShapes today’s market or technology.
  1. 5 minIntroduction

    Ian introduces the guests and the episode’s innovation theme, framing the technology shift under discussion.

  2. 10 minAct I — The Past

    The historical guest explains the prior pivot: what changed, and what leap in quality or access it unlocked.

  3. 10 minAct II — The Present

    The operating guest analyzes how today’s technology is reshaping the relationship between content, creator, and customer.

  4. 10 minAct III — Future Synthesis

    Both guests commit to a probability forecast on a defined scenario; the audience casts a Community Forecast against it.

03 / Season One

Eight transitions. Eight forecasts.

Each episode follows one line of Hollywood’s evolution from an earlier pivot to the present, then closes on a forecast question tied to its theme. Questions for Episodes 02–08 may be refined with each episode’s guests.

  1. Episode 01

    Customer Evolution

    Theatergoer → digital viewer

    The 75-year arc from the 1947 theatergoer to today’s digital viewer, and the strategies studios used to capture, retain, and personalize the audience.

    Read the Episode 01 question ↓

  2. Episode 02

    Media Supply Chain Evolution

    Studio lot → fragmented pipeline

    From vertically integrated studios to decentralized streaming and gaming pipelines—and the “Complexity Tax” of managing a fragmented distribution landscape.

    Forecast question for Episode 02

    By December 31, 2028, will two major U.S. studios publicly confirm production use of an AI agent that can initiate media-supply-chain actions without per-action human approval?

    Threshold
    Two studios confirm qualifying use.
    Deadline
    December 31, 2028
    Evidence
    Public disclosure and established trade reporting.

  3. Episode 03

    Creator Evolution

    Contract player → sovereign creator

    From the studio-controlled Contract Player system to the platform-agnostic Sovereign Creator, and the early emergence of the Synthetic Idol.

    Forecast question for Episode 03

    By December 31, 2030, will a fully synthetic performer receive top billing in a film released in at least 2,000 U.S. theaters?

    Threshold
    Top billing in a 2,000-theater release.
    Deadline
    December 31, 2030
    Evidence
    Distributor disclosure and established reporting.

  4. Episode 04

    Content Evolution

    Finished film → adaptive story

    From the feature film as a fixed, finished event to a composable, adaptive experience shaped by user co-creation and real-time narrative adjustment.

    Forecast question for Episode 04

    By December 31, 2029, will a film with audience-selected narrative branches receive a release in at least 1,000 U.S. theaters?

    Threshold
    A qualifying film reaches 1,000 U.S. theaters.
    Deadline
    December 31, 2029
    Evidence
    Distributor disclosure and established reporting.

  5. Episode 05

    Commercial Evolution

    Syndication → shoppable story

    From mid-century broadcast syndication through the subscription era to AI-driven advertising designed to remove friction between story and sale.

    Forecast question for Episode 05

    By December 31, 2028, will at least one of Netflix, Disney+, HBO Max, Peacock, and Paramount+ launch click-to-buy product placement inside scripted programming?

    Threshold
    One named service launches the feature.
    Deadline
    December 31, 2028
    Evidence
    Product documentation and established reporting.

  6. Episode 06

    Audio Evolution

    Synchronized sound → adaptive soundscape

    From synchronized sound to immersive, object-based, and eventually adaptive soundscapes that change how audiences experience story and presence.

    Forecast question for Episode 06

    By December 31, 2029, will at least two of Netflix, Disney+, HBO Max, Peacock, and Paramount+ let viewers personalize dialogue, music, or effects levels for a scripted title?

    Threshold
    Two named services offer qualifying controls.
    Deadline
    December 31, 2029
    Evidence
    Product documentation and established reporting.

  7. Episode 07

    VFX Evolution

    In-camera illusion → generative world

    From Georges Méliès’ in-camera illusions to today’s virtual-production factories, and on to prompt-based, generative worlds.

    Forecast question for Episode 07

    By December 31, 2028, will a major studio publicly state that generative tools created more than half of the final VFX shots in a 2,000-theater release?

    Threshold
    More than half of final shots in a qualifying release.
    Deadline
    December 31, 2028
    Evidence
    Studio disclosure and established reporting.

  8. Episode 08

    Animation Evolution

    Theater filler → global visual language

    From theater-filler shorts to the dominant visual language of global digital media—closing on the legal and ethical frontiers of generative animation.

    Forecast question for Episode 08

    By December 31, 2029, will a final U.S. appellate decision hold that training a generative model on unlicensed copyrighted audiovisual works is not fair use?

    Threshold
    A final appellate decision makes the specified holding.
    Deadline
    December 31, 2029
    Evidence
    The public court opinion and cited case record.

04 / Episode 01 · Customer Evolution · Act III

When does the ad tier become the main tier?

Subscription streaming sold viewers an ad-free alternative. Episode 01 follows the audience from the 1947 theatergoer to today’s digital viewer—and closes on whether advertising becomes the way most of them pay.

The forecast question

Will at least three of Netflix, Disney+, HBO Max, Peacock, and Paramount+ report more U.S. subscribers on ad-supported plans than ad-free plans by December 31, 2029?

Threshold
Three named services report the crossover.
Deadline
December 31, 2029
Evidence
Company reporting and established trade reporting.

      05 / The prediction market

      Expert Alpha versus the crowd.

      Every finale turns speculation into a number. Each forecast question is developed with DEG sub-committees around a theme that matters to studio, platform, technology, and creative leaders.

      1. 01Expert Alpha

        Both guests put a professional percentage forecast on the episode’s defined scenario.

      2. 02Community Forecast

        The audience forecasts the same question on the same terms, so expert and crowd consensus sit side by side.

      3. 03Market Update

        Later episodes revisit earlier questions, show how consensus has moved, and grade each forecast against the outcome.

      The aim is calibration, not crowning winners: a recurring, measurable read on where the industry thinks it is headed—and how well it called the last turn.
