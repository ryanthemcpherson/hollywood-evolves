# Website Direction

## Positioning

**Hollywood Evolves is not a futurism blog.** It is an executive conversation that uses Hollywood's history to make measurable claims about what happens next.

Ian's credible lane is operator-convener. TMT Insights describes him as Head of Business Development with 25+ years in media technology and an 11-year AWS tenure spanning industry marketing, strategic partnerships, business development, and agentic AI in the media supply chain.[1] TMT's appointment announcement says he now leads partnership strategy and go-to-market expansion after leading global media-and-entertainment business development strategy at AWS.[2] AWS previously identified him as its global business-development lead for media supply chain, archive, and data science/analytics.[4]

That background supports a sharper territory and host statement:

> **Hollywood’s operating system:** how AI, cloud infrastructure, archives, streaming economics, new distribution models, and changing audience behavior alter what gets made, how it reaches viewers, and how media assets generate value.

> **Ian McPherson has spent more than 25 years inside the systems that create, move, and monetize entertainment. Hollywood Evolves brings the people who built the last transition together with the operators building the next one—and asks them to put a number on what happens next.**

Because Ian's current role is commercial and partnership-facing, the site and show notes should disclose relevant employer, sponsor, client, and guest relationships and distinguish Ian's editorial view from positions held by TMT Insights, DEG, guests' employers, or his former employer.

## Recommended homepage

### 1. Hero

Eyebrow: `A podcast and public forecast ledger`

Headline:

> **The technologies that changed Hollywood—and the odds on what changes next.**

Support:

> Ian McPherson brings an institutional voice and a current operator together for one focused conversation: what changed, what is changing, and what each believes will happen next.

Primary action: `View the first forecast`
Secondary action: `Explore season one`

Avoid “We've seen this movie before.” It is clever but familiar, and the existing prototype spends too long explaining the premise before giving visitors something to do.[8]

### 2. Current forecast

One large card, not a carousel:

- question
- closing date
- Guest / Community / Research System probabilities
- community sample size
- `Make your forecast`
- `See the evidence and rules`

Before real data exists, show **Preview — not yet open**. Never manufacture aired status, percentages, trend values, or comments.

Written commentary will require Sign in with LinkedIn using OpenID Connect. This authenticates control of a LinkedIn account; it is not proof of real-world identity. Any verified-industry designation requires a separate editorial review, and submissions remain closed until moderation, rate limiting, abuse controls, secure sessions, and updated privacy disclosures are implemented.

### 3. Episode format

A single combined split chapter on desktop, stacked on mobile:

`THE PAST → THE PRESENT → THE FORECAST`

One concise subject statement and controlled diagram per historical act, followed by an explicit jump to the forecast. No generic feature-card grid.

### 4. Season one

Lead with a controlled 3D discovery field for the early pool of eight approved questions, with browser-local `YES / NO` calls and stable links to their contracts. Explicitly distinguish this pool from the three-forecast episode mix: one structural, one operating, and one fast-resolving. Follow with eight compact rows containing number, theme, and honest question status. Do not write eight long AI summaries on the homepage.

### 5. Why Ian / About the host

Use a strong real portrait, a compact biography, and a few specific credentials. Ian's experience includes executive marketing, product management, business development, media supply chains, cloud adoption, AI-enabled media workflows, and a USC master's in communications.[3]

### 6. Method

Show four verbs:

`DEFINE → FORECAST → UPDATE → SCORE`

Link to the full method and resolution policy. This is the trust section, not an “AI-powered” sales section.

### 7. Partners and distribution

Only show DEG, TMT Insights, Apple, Spotify, or YouTube logos/status when those relationships and links are approved for publication. The supplied executive brief describes a DEG partnership, but the site should not infer approval from a planning document.

## Visual system

Direction: **trade publication + market terminal**, not AI startup.

- Warm paper background, near-black type, one signal color (oxide red or cobalt).
- Editorial serif for headlines; plain grotesk sans for data and controls.
- Thin rules, tabular numbers, restrained motion.
- Historical imagery or technical artifacts used as evidence, not decorative generated collages.
- Forecast chart resembles an annotated ledger, not a crypto ticker.
- No glassmorphism, purple gradients, glowing orbs, floating cards, fake activity, or stock “future city” imagery.

## Pages for MVP

1. `/` — premise, current forecast, season, host, method
2. `/episodes` — eight-episode slate and published episodes
3. `/forecasts/[slug]` — question, probabilities, evidence, update history, resolution contract
4. `/method` — methodology, scoring, corrections, data/privacy policy
5. `/about` — Ian, TMT, DEG (after approval)

## Data credibility rules

- Every forecast shows its timestamp and evidence cutoff.
- Community number always shows sample size.
- “Trend” is displayed only when backed by a defined quantitative series.
- Model output is labeled `Research System`, not `AI consensus` or `truth`.
- Corrections append to history; they do not silently replace old text.
- Demo content is watermarked `Illustrative preview` and cannot be mistaken for a live episode.
